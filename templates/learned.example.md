Written by li-audit from real account data. Read by li-post, li-plan and li-hooks.
Evidence from this account outranks generic advice.

## Audit, 2026-10-01 (4 posts with numbers, 2,166 followers)

| Post | Impressions | Reactions | Comments | Reposts | Eng. rate | Reach vs followers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Most QA portfolios are a list of tools" (link preview to portfolio) | 81,200 | 179 | 8 | 5 | 0.24% | 37x |
| "Why I Stopped Prompting AI" (article link) | 2,514 | 5 | 3 | 0 | 0.32% | 1.2x |

## What the winners share
- [evidence] The one post that broke out, 37x follower count, was about a shared
  professional frustration ("most QA portfolios are a list of tools"), not about a
  tool. Identity and career beat implementation detail for reach on this account.
- [evidence] Its hook is a contrarian claim about something the reader already
  believes they know, then a personal correction. Two short lines, no setup.
- [evidence] It carried a link preview card and still reached 81,200. The generic
  "links cost 50-70% reach" rule did not hold here. Treat the link rule as a
  default to beat, not a law: a post strong enough to be reshared outruns it.
- [evidence] 5 reposts on that post against 0-1 elsewhere. Reposts, not likes,
  are what took it past the network.

## What underperforms
- [evidence] Tool launches reach 0.07x of followers. Announcing a thing I built
  interests far fewer people than the problem the thing solves.
- [evidence] Deep technical posts (green != correct) hold a high engagement rate,
  3.0%, among the few who see them, but barely leave the network. They are worth
  posting for credibility, not for reach.
- [evidence] High engagement rate on a small post is not success. 3.2% of 157 is
  five people. Rate and reach have to be read together.

## What to do next
- [action] Lead with the problem and the shared frustration, not the artefact.
  Mention the tool as the answer, late, after the reader already agrees there is
  a problem.
- [action] Write for the repost, not the like. Ask what would make a peer send
  this to someone else.
- [action] Keep technical posts, but expect them to serve credibility, and do not
  judge them by reach.
